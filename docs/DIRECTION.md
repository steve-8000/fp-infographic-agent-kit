# Research to direction to our language

The compiler makes a frame consistent. It cannot make it worth looking at. That comes from
deciding what this particular story needs — and the point of this process is that the
decision is recorded and checkable rather than asserted.

## 1. Read the subject before the references

What is the reader's actual question, what are the source facts, and what would change
their mind? A frame that answers nothing is not rescued by art direction.

## 2. Search the open web

`fp_brief` returns starting queries. Search outside this repository and outside the
FOUR PILLARS file, then open the results and look at them. Record source, date, what was
on the screen, and what must change for it to become ours. `fp_direction` marks a
reference without a date and an observation as unverified: usable as inspiration, never
citable, and it warns when every reference is internal.

Our own published frames are useful for calibration, not for direction. They already
show that the pack is a default rather than a ceiling — the same file uses H1 at 50, 54
and 60, and places cards outside the nominal safe zone — but a direction assembled only
from them reproduces the last frame.

## 3. Translate into pack surfaces

A direction is only real when it moves something the renderer can express:
`style.roles.*`, `colorMode`, `ramp`, `accents`, `blocks.shape`, `density`,
`safeVariant`, `chrome.titleBlock`. Anything else is content or taste.

This is the step that turns "inspired by" into our language. A reference supplies a
principle; the decision states the value, in our tokens, with the reference named.

## 4. Build, then reconcile

Compose with `blocks`, render, and compare against the statement rather than against the
references. If the frame is only defensible by pointing at a reference, the direction did
not survive translation.

## 5. What a direction may never move

Facts, the safe zone, the pack minimum size, chrome, palette membership. Those are
correctness, not style. A direction that requires one of them is a new theme pack, and it
should be proposed as one.
